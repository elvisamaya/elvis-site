Systolic-Array AI Accelerator

Verilog · Python/NumPy · Yosys · OpenLane · SkyWater 130nm

An int8 matrix-multiply accelerator built around a 4×4 output-stationary systolic array, the same architecture as Google's TPU. It's memory-mapped onto my pipelined RISC-V processor, and the CPU drives it with ordinary loads and stores.

I ran a quantized handwritten-digit classifier on it: every hardware layer output matched NumPy bit for bit. Then I took the design from RTL to a placed-and-routed SkyWater 130nm layout with OpenLane: 0 DRC and LVS errors, and 100 MHz timing met at the typical corner.

Verification compared about 3.1 million multiply-accumulates against a NumPy golden model, and all 8 deliberately planted bugs were caught.

87.6×faster than the CPU alone
95.8%int8 accuracy, bit-exact
~300 MHzarray, pre-layout estimate
0 / 0DRC / LVS errors

Pipelined RISC-V Processor

Verilog · RV32I · Python

Single-cycle and five-stage RV32I cores, built to compare how pipeline hazards affect performance. The pipelined core forwards ALU results, stalls on load-use dependencies, and flushes younger instructions on taken branches.

I wrote my own assembler and instruction-set simulator as a golden model. Every program's final registers, memory and instruction count must match it exactly, including thousands of randomly generated instructions.

Vital Sign Monitoring with mmWave Radar

TI IWR6843ISK · 77 GHz FMCW radar · signal processing · Stanford, May–June 2026

A contactless heart-rate and respiration monitor. Instead of wearable or contact sensors like PPG, it measures sub-millimeter chest displacement from the phase of the radar signal.

I derived and implemented the full signal-processing pipeline (range-FFT, DC offset correction, phase extraction and unwrapping, and slow-time FFT) and designed the chirp parameters to reach under 0.1 Hz frequency resolution. That's enough to separate breathing (0.2–0.5 Hz) from heart rate (1–1.6 Hz). Across multiple trials it reliably detected respiratory rate (14–19 bpm) and heart rate (61–80 bpm) from 1 m away.

Butter Bot

STM32 · ESP32S3 camera · I2C · UART · PWM · 3D printing · in progress, Fall 2026

An autonomous mini forklift inspired by the Butter Robot from Rick and Morty. It uses a camera to find a stick of butter, slides its forks under a small pallet, lifts it, and delivers it to a red drop-off marker.

An STM32 Nucleo-L4R5ZI-P runs the robot as an interrupt-driven state machine. It receives butter and marker positions from a XIAO ESP32S3 Sense camera over UART, reads heading from an LSM303AGR magnetometer over I2C, and drives two DC gearmotors and an SG92R servo with timer PWM. The 25-part 3D-printed body includes a parallel-link forklift that lifts the forks 30 mm while keeping them level.
